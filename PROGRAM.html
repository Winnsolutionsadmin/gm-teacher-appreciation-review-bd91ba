Growing Mindfully Teacher Appreciation Program

Internal working doc for Jarred and Ashley. Designed 2026-09-02 on Jarred's direction: "it's just teacher appreciation. Starbucks orders, a bought lunch, team success, aligned on positive parent experiences, new and old." This replaces the heavier July "Community Moments" package; the research and counsel work behind that package is kept in reference/ and this design takes its three safety rails and nothing else.

Status: DESIGN. Nothing launched. No family message sent. No staff announcement drafted (that is Jarred's, in his words, per the standing rule).

In one sentence

Every positive review a family leaves for Growing Mindfully adds a star to the jar; at each of five marks (1, 3, 5, 10, 25) the whole team gets a celebration, delivered by Jarred or Ashley on a Friday. The first mark is one star, so the first win comes fast.

Why this shape is safe (and the three rails that keep it safe)

The July research (reference/research-report-20260723.md) found the risky shape is paying individual employees per five-star review, or having staff solicit. A whole-team lunch when the school hits a milestone is not that. What can make it risky is behavior leaking outward, so:

  1. Teachers never ask. Only Jarred or Ashley ever mention Google to a family or send a review link. Teachers never ask a family for a review, never ask to be named, never mention the jar, the treats, or the wheel to a family. (Google's Apr 2026 policy bans staff review quotas and asking reviews to name staff; Yelp bans asking at all.)
  2. Families are never told reviews buy treats. The family-facing invitation is a neutral thank-you plus a link (copy below). Six of six counsel models called "your review funds staff treats" a mistake: it reads as an incentive and invites platform and FTC scrutiny.
  3. Yelp is never asked. Yelp reviews that arrive on their own count in the jar. We respond to every Yelp review, keep photos fresh, and never solicit there.

Everything else is just appreciation.

What counts as a star

  • One new five-star review on Google or Yelp, from any family: current, new, or alumni. One review = one star, never five reviews per star. (Jarred's stated bar, restated to Ashley 2026-09-02, is five stars. Recommendation: count 4 too. A 4-star review with a warm story is a positive parent experience, and counting it removes any reason for anyone to fish for the fifth star. Jarred's call; the default in this doc is 4-5.)
  • One review = one star, no matter how long it is or whether it names a teacher. No bonus for names (that is what keeps rail 1 honest).
  • A review that later gets filtered or removed by the platform keeps its star. Never claw back.
  • A private thank-you note or Brightwheel message from a family does NOT count toward the jar (keeps the count simple) but gets read aloud at the huddle all the same.
  • A negative review adds nothing and costs nothing. Jarred replies to the family within 2 business days. At the huddle the team hears the underlying need, not a dramatic reading, names one thing to try, and the next treat proceeds unchanged.

The ladder (the star jar)

Every positive review is a star in the jar. Five marks, five celebrations, and the first one lands almost immediately so the team feels the win before the mechanic even settles in. Costs are for the whole 23-person team (scaling toward 30).

Stars Celebration Approx cost
1 Coffee and pastries morning: the first win, celebrated the same week $80-100
3 Starbucks run: everyone's order, Jarred delivers Friday morning $140-180
5 Bought lunch: platters, or Ashley cooks; the team eats in shifts $250-350
10 Catered lunch: Jarred and Ashley cover rooms so the team eats together in two shifts $400-500
25 Grand prize: team dinner out, or a gift card each (Jarred's call) $1,000-2,000

After the grand prize the jar empties and the ladder starts over, so the small wins keep coming. Time gifts cost nothing and rank highest with tired ECE staff: "Jarred covers your room for an hour" and "leave 30 minutes early Friday, staggered" can be folded into any mark, one classroom at a time so ratios hold. Marks are Jarred's to set ("we could make it whatever we want").

Budget: one full cycle (25 stars) costs roughly $1,900-3,100 all in. At the realistic pace of 4-8 reviews a month a cycle takes 3-6 months, so the run rate is about $400-700 a month. It is a fixed appreciation line, not performance pay. If the pace runs hot the circuit breaker (below) slows the asks, not the celebrations.

Where the reviews come from (the ask track)

Reviews should come from families who were asked neutrally, at a steady pace, by Jarred or Ashley only, plus whatever arrives on its own.

  • New families: one invitation at about 60-90 days after start. By then the family has a real experience to describe and the honeymoon is over.
  • Alumni and departing families: one invitation in the child's last week, or at the kindergarten send-off. Departures are the emotional peak; state-worker families churn, so this is where "old" parent experiences live.
  • Everyone else: enrollment-anniversary cohorts, about one cohort a week (metronome/cohort-metronome.sh prints this week's cohort from the Solace mirror, read only; data caveat: only 12 of 75 children had enrollment_date in July, DOB fallback was used).
  • Rules: one ask per family per 12 months. A family that does not respond is left in peace. No filtering by how happy a family seems (that is review gating, banned by Google and flagged by the FTC). Individually addressed email from jarred@growingmindfully.org, never a BCC blast.
  • Circuit breaker: if 3 or more new Google reviews land within any 7 days, pause outbound invitations for 14 days. Google freezes profiles on velocity spikes; too fast is a warning here.
  • Send gate: every family send is drafted, then approved by Jarred per message, then sent. Nothing automates a send.

The family-facing text (Tier 2, gated, use verbatim)

Counsel-approved paragraph (5 of 6 models), no stars, no treats, no Yelp:

At Growing Mindfully, we try to notice the small moments that shape a child's day. If something has stayed with you, whether it felt joyful, difficult, or somewhere in between, we would be grateful to hear it. You can share privately with Ashley or Jarred, or leave an honest Google review if you would rather share publicly. Every story helps us listen, learn, and care for our community more thoughtfully.

Placements, subject lines, the cohort email, the departing/alumni farewell email, and the review-reply rules are staged in copy/staged-family-copy.md. The lobby sign and story cards (print/) carry the paragraph with no Google link: the lobby is for stories, the link only travels to families who are off premises and unpressured.

The Friday huddle (about five minutes)

  1. Jarred or Ashley reads this week's new reviews aloud (the warm parts; teacher names as the family wrote them are fine to read internally) and drops the stars in the jar.
  2. If a milestone was hit, the treat is announced and scheduled (or is already sitting there).
  3. Optional: a time gift (Jarred covers a room for an hour), one room at a time.
  4. If a hard review arrived: the need, one thing to try, no blame, treat unchanged.

The jar, the star-board poster, or the review wall (print/review-wall.html, recommended) lives in the staff room. Jarred keeps the count. No child ever places a star (see decision log).

Response discipline (the part that grows Yelp without asking)

  • Reply to every Google and Yelp review within 2 business days, personally, in the GM voice. Yelp's own data: responses to critical reviews earn rating upgrades.
  • Keep GBP and Yelp photos fresh (classroom, playground, teachers at work; real assets only).
  • Never reference licensing in any reply. No em dashes.

Launch

  • Week 1: Jarred announces it at a huddle, in his own words, and hands out the first treat before explaining the mechanic (the first star, and the coffee-and-pastries morning, will land within days of launch). He states the one rule teachers own: never ask, never mention it to families.
  • Week 2: The paragraph goes out once to all families via Brightwheel (Jarred approves the send). Lobby card goes up if Jarred approves printing.
  • Week 3 on: Friday rhythm runs. Cohort invitations begin, about one a week, breaker armed.
  • Month 3: Quick check: stars collected, every mark celebrated the week it was hit, response times, and a read of whether any family-facing language drifted toward "five stars" or "mention Ms. X" (if so, pause asks and reset).

Legal footnote (plain)

FTC 16 CFR 465 restricts incentives to reviewers conditioned on sentiment, insider reviews without disclosure, and review suppression. It does not squarely address an internal team-appreciation program, and the July research found no enforcement action either way. Exposure comes from behavior leaking outward (staff soliciting, families told reviews buy treats, asking on Yelp), which rails 1-3 close. Penalties, for scale, run up to $53,088 per violation. Staff or family members must not post reviews without a disclosed relationship.
