Starbucks tier · recommended

Time gifts cost nothing and rank highest with tired ECE staff: "Jarred covers your room for an hour" and "leave 30 minutes early Friday, staggered" stay on the Friday wheel as bonus prizes, one classroom at a time so ratios hold. At the realistic pace (4-8 reviews a month) the whole thing runs $250-450 a month, $3-5k a year: a fixed appreciation line, not performance pay.

What counts as a stone

One review, one stone

A new 4 or 5 star review on Google or Yelp from any family: new, current, or alumni. No bonus for length, no bonus for naming a teacher. That is what keeps the first rail honest.

Never clawed back

If a platform later filters or removes a review, the stone stays.

Private notes

A thank-you note or Brightwheel message does not go in the jar, so the count stays legible, but it gets read aloud at the huddle all the same.

A hard review

Adds nothing, costs nothing. Jarred replies within 2 business days. The team hears the need, names one thing to try, no blame. The next treat proceeds unchanged.

The three rails

The July research (78 sources, 6-model counsel panel) found the risky shape is paying individual employees per five-star review, or having staff solicit. A team lunch at a milestone is not that. What could make it risky is behavior leaking outward, so three rules carry the legal weight.

Teachers never ask

Only Jarred or Ashley ever mention Google to a family or send a link. Teachers never ask for a review, never ask to be named, never mention the jar or the treats to a family. Google bans staff review quotas and asking reviews to name staff; Yelp bans asking at all.

Families are never told

The invitation is a neutral thank-you plus a link. Six of six counsel models called "your review funds staff treats" a mistake: it reads as an incentive and invites platform and FTC scrutiny.

Yelp is never asked

Yelp reviews that arrive on their own count. We respond to every Yelp review, keep photos fresh, and never solicit there.

If any of these show up in a family-facing sentence, pause the asks and reset:

"five stars" / "5-star""mention Ms. or Mr. [teacher]""it helps the teachers get lunch""leave us a Yelp review"any batch blast of review links

Rules: one ask per family per 12 months; non-responders are left in peace; no filtering by how happy a family seems (that is review gating); individually addressed from jarred@growingmindfully.org, never BCC; every send approved by Jarred per message, nothing automates a send. Circuit breaker: three or more new Google reviews within any 7 days pauses outbound invitations for 14 days. Google freezes profiles on velocity spikes; too fast is a warning, not a win.

The only family-facing text, counsel-approved 5 of 6, used verbatim in every placement:

At Growing Mindfully, we try to notice the small moments that shape a child's day. If something has stayed with you, whether it felt joyful, difficult, or somewhere in between, we would be grateful to hear it. You can share privately with Ashley or Jarred, or leave an honest Google review if you would rather share publicly. Every story helps us listen, learn, and care for our community more thoughtfully.

Friday, five minutes

  1. Read and drop Jarred or Ashley reads this week's new reviews aloud (the warm parts) and drops the stones in.
  2. Milestone If one was hit, announce the treat and schedule it, or it is already sitting there.
  3. Wheel, optional Spin for the flavor or a bonus time gift. The wheel page says nothing about reviews.
  4. Hard review The need, one thing to try, no blame, treat unchanged.

  1. Week 1 Jarred announces at a huddle and hands out the first Starbucks run before explaining anything. 27 existing reviews' worth of goodwill seeds the jar.
  2. Week 2 The paragraph goes out once via Brightwheel after Jarred approves the send. Lobby sign and cards go up if he approves printing.
  3. Week 3 on Friday rhythm runs. Cohort and farewell invitations begin, breaker armed.
  4. Month 3 Stones collected, floor honored every month, response times, and a language check against the kill phrases.

In your words

The team announcement is not drafted here. For it to stay inside the rails, it needs to carry these five things, however you say them:

  • The jar and the ladder: what fills it, what it pays out, that it never resets, and that the floor is guaranteed.
  • The one rule teachers own: never ask a family for a review, never ask to be named, never mention the jar or the treats to a family. Everything about asking is yours and Ashley's.
  • A hard review costs the team nothing.
  • The first treat is already in hand (hand it out before you explain).
  • Yelp is off the table for everyone, including friends and family of staff.

Legal footnote, plain

FTC 16 CFR 465 restricts incentives to reviewers conditioned on sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews, and review suppression. It does not squarely address an internal team-appreciation program, and the July research found no enforcement action either way. Exposure comes from behavior leaking outward, which the three rails close. Penalties, for scale, run up to $53,088 per violation. Staff and family members must not post reviews without a disclosed relationship.
