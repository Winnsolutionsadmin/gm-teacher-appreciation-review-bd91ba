Three rules

Teachers never ask

Only Jarred or Ashley ever mention Google to a family. Teachers never ask for a review, never ask to be named, never mention the jar.

Families are told plainly, once

Jarred's call (v6): a flyer at check-in explains the jar. It says every review adds a star, never "five-star": Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, and with 27 of 27 at five stars there is nothing to gain by saying it.

Yelp is never asked

Yelp reviews that arrive on their own count. The flyer carries a small "Find us on Yelp" badge (allowed) and no Yelp ask or QR (not allowed: solicited Yelp reviews get demoted and the profile gets a Consumer Alert). The QR is Google only.

How stars arrive

A star is one new review on Google or Yelp, from any family, new or alumni. One review, one star. Never taken back. The flyer promises "every review", so the jar should count every review; five-star-only is on the list below but it would make the flyer untrue.

Families learn about it from the check-in flyer (Google logo, QR to the review page) and the take-away cards in a holder beside it. The ask by email is one warm note from Jarred, once per family per year: new families at 60-90 days, leaving families in their last week, everyone else on their enrollment anniversary. Same paragraph every time, one link, individually sent, Jarred approves each one. If reviews spike (3+ in a week), asks pause two weeks.

Friday takes five minutes: read the new reviews aloud, drop the stars, hand out whatever mark was hit. A hard review costs the team nothing; Jarred replies to the family within two days.

Jar, star board, or review wall?

The jar

A real jar in the staff room, one star dropped in per review, marks taped on the side. Tactile, and the count is obvious from across the room.

The star board

A poster of 25 outline stars in a pyramid, one colored in per review, the five marks numbered. Preview the poster. Ashley: "it's really cute. It's actually very preschool, just for adults."

The review wall recommended

A 24x36 poster with 25 prefilled slots. Each review is printed on a 4x6 card, as the family wrote it, and pinned into the next slot; the five marks are outlined. The appreciation is the words, not the tally. When slot 25 fills, the cards go in a book and the wall resets. Preview the poster · card maker.

Whichever one: it hangs in the staff room, never the lobby (families must never see reviews paired with rewards). No per-teacher tally, ever; cards carry teacher names as written, the wall counts only for the team. No words on it addressed to staff beyond the numbers; any caption is Jarred's. Kids do not place stars or cards (Ashley: manipulative, and too young to care). Dropped.

Check-in flyer (supersedes the lobby sign question)

Jarred's direction, v6: families are told. The check-in flyer does that in variant-A spirit with two corrections that are policy, not taste: it says "every review" (not "five-star"), and Yelp is a badge, not an ask. The earlier sign variants stay on the board for reference: three rewrites, side by side with the trade-off under each. The tension to decide: the counsel rail says families are never told reviews buy treats; the sign Jarred described tells them exactly that. That rail is about pressure and optics, not law (the FTC and Google restrict incentives to the reviewer, not to staff), so it is his call, made with eyes open.

A · Plain

"Every five-star review from a happy family puts a star on our teachers' board, and every star earns them a little celebration." Says it outright. Fails the lint by design.

B · Thank a teacher

Never mentions reviews. Kind words of any kind fill the star board; the Google link only ever travels in Jarred's yearly email. Passes.

C · Middle recommended

Names the star board and names Google once as one way to share, never the treats. Families learn their words fill the board, not what a full board buys. Passes.
